# Architecture decisions

## ADR-001 — Separate application

Broker Growth Desk is a separate application that selectively reuses the approved visual foundation. Construction domain code is not copied or renamed.

## ADR-002 — Organisation tenant boundary

Every tenant-owned record carries `organisationId`; every repository operation requires an organisation-scoped `TenantContext` and validates membership. Brokerage remains the mortgage-facing label.

## ADR-003 — Capability-based permissions

Membership positions define fixture bundles only. Shared membership is industry-neutral; the Broker position lives in a mortgage profile. Domain decisions inspect typed capabilities, tenant match, ownership and assignment state.

## ADR-004 — Explicit mortgage lead lifecycle

Transitions are enumerated in domain code. Converted is terminal in Milestone 1. Closed leads may only be deliberately reopened into Nurture.

## ADR-005 — Appointment transition

Creating a discovery appointment is allowed only from an eligible status, moves the lead to Discovery booked and records both transition and appointment events.

## ADR-006 — Fixture state

One in-memory repository powers the prototype and resets on reload. It must not be described as persistence.

## ADR-007 — No financial assessment data

Milestone 1 collects only data required for lead response and appointment operations. Assessment, comparison, advice and financial-document data are outside the product boundary.

## ADR-008 — Household-centred client book

Household is the operational client aggregate. Client people are linked through explicit member relationships so single and joint clients are represented without duplicating household-level ownership, referral and review work.

## ADR-009 — Explicit atomic conversion

Converted is removed from generic lead transitions. Conversion is a dedicated transaction-shaped operation that records duplicate handling, consent provenance, audit activity and the resulting household link. In-memory rollback is a prototype simulation only.

## ADR-010 — Retention-only loan boundary

Minimal loan records contain identifiers, lender name, purpose, lifecycle status and review dates. Balance, rate, affordability, credit, recommendation and application data are prohibited.

## ADR-011 — Deterministic review generation

Annual-review and fixed-rate-expiry opportunities are generated by a pure service with an injected clock. Idempotency is based on loan, opportunity type and trigger date. Generated work starts unassigned so assignment is deliberate.

## ADR-012 — Duplicate and consent provenance policy

Duplicate matching is tenant-scoped and advisory: normalised email and phone are strong signals and exact full name is weaker. Users choose to create a new household, add the lead person to an existing household or cancel; automatic merging is prohibited. Consent copies preserve channel status, source and original timestamp.

## ADR-013 — Shared platform with a mortgage vertical

Industry-neutral contracts live in `src/platform`; mortgage meaning lives in `src/verticals/mortgage`. Platform code cannot import a vertical. Features and the registry are composition layers. This preserves one deployable application while making future vertical ownership explicit.

## ADR-014 — Contact is shared; Household is not

Contact is a reusable person record. Mortgage Household groups contacts and owns mortgage retention relationships, so it remains vertical-specific. Future verticals may introduce different aggregates without changing Contact.

## ADR-015 — Opportunity mechanism versus mortgage meaning

The platform owns a typed opportunity foundation and lifecycle assertion. Mortgage owns annual-review and fixed-rate-expiry types, date windows, generation, household and loan relationships.

## ADR-016 — Generic campaign orchestration with vertical registrations

The platform owns campaign lifecycle, immutable versions, snapshots, shared eligibility, suppression and approval. Each vertical registers typed purpose/source metadata plus runtime audience providers and merge resolvers with explicit dependencies. Mortgage owns annual-review and fixed-rate-expiry meaning. This lets another vertical add a maintenance reminder without mortgage imports or platform campaign changes.

## ADR-017 — Remain a modular monolith

No packages or monorepo are introduced while there is one real consumer. Extraction requires a second deployable consumer, independent release needs, divergent ownership/build pipelines or dependency boundaries that cannot be enforced in this repository.

## ADR-018 — Campaign approval is not delivery

Approved freezes a reviewed campaign version, stores a freshly evaluated audience snapshot and records actor, time and counts. It creates no delivery instruction or status. Sending, scheduling, queues, exports and provider integrations remain absent; any future messaging adapter requires a separately reviewed boundary and final eligibility design.

## ADR-019 — Conservative, explainable eligibility

Email and SMS require an explicit channel consent status of Recorded. Suppression, opt-out, Not recorded consent, vertical purpose rules, address validity, tenant mismatch and duplicates produce structured reasons. No campaign-level override exists. Submission and approval re-run current checks with an injected clock and preserve prior snapshots.

## ADR-020 — Approval references a reviewed audience snapshot

An approval request names the exact audience snapshot reviewed. The repository freshly evaluates and compares a canonical material audience projection. If it changed, the new snapshot and structured changes are retained but approval is not recorded; the revised snapshot requires another explicit approval. Successful approval records both the reviewed and final snapshot IDs. Evaluation timestamps and generated record IDs are excluded from material comparison.

## ADR-021 — Consent and suppression are append-only evidence

Suppression resolves the latest effective entry independently for organisation, contact and contact-channel scope; Lifted neutralises an earlier Active entry, future entries are ignored and deterministic timestamp tie-breaks apply. An Opted out to Recorded consent change requires external source, effective time, evidence note and explicit external-action confirmation with actor provenance. Neither record type represents a communication performed by the prototype.

## ADR-022 — Supabase for the pilot trust boundary

Use Supabase Postgres, Auth, SSR cookie helpers, migrations and RLS for the pilot because one local toolchain supplies relational persistence, invite-only identity and database authorization while retaining standard PostgreSQL constraints. This is not a commitment to dashboard-managed schema or browser-owned domain logic: migrations remain authoritative and server services remain responsible for lifecycle decisions.

## ADR-023 — Explicit temporary dual runtime

`fixture` remains the default walkthrough runtime. `supabase` never falls back to fixtures and exposes only workflow slices that have verified server repositories and guarded operation boundaries. The active-organisation cookie is a preference only. Milestones 4B–4F progressively narrowed the original identity-only boundary while preserving explicit fixture-only areas such as campaigns.

## ADR-024 — Relational capabilities and database tenant consistency

Capabilities are rows linked to an organisation membership and seeded from supported application capability names. They are never sourced from editable user metadata. Every tenant relationship uses `organisation_id` in database constraints so application mistakes cannot connect records across brokerages. Shared platform and mortgage tables remain explicit; no EAV vertical store is introduced.

## ADR-025 — Append-only protection is layered, not absolute

RLS and grants deny ordinary update/delete access to history and immutable campaign records, with triggers protecting append-only/frozen records where justified. These controls do not claim immutability against database administrators. Draft campaign versions remain mutable through future controlled services.

## ADR-026 — Dedicated hosted development verification

Milestone 4A.1 uses the hosted `AI Native Operations` project (`fgqgcikmaccjamwocpat`) as development-only infrastructure with fictional data. Supabase's main-branch `production` label does not change that application environment classification. Every hosted command must verify both name and reference; destructive resets and undocumented dashboard schema changes are prohibited.

## ADR-027 — Read-only tables with separately controlled operations

RLS read policies remain capability- and organisation-scoped, and ordinary authenticated clients receive no direct workflow table mutation privileges. Milestones 4B–4F introduced lead, conversion, Client Book, retention and referral changes one operation slice at a time with identity, membership, capability, ownership, transition, tenant, atomicity and audit validation. Campaign drafting, submission and approval remain fixture-only pending their own reviewed persistent boundary.

## ADR-028 — Hosted schema types and parity tests

Database types are generated from the exact hosted development project and consumed only at Supabase and persistence boundaries. Runtime domain-value constants and migration constraints are compared by automated tests. The 60-assertion pgTAP suite remains transaction wrapped with a fixed plan for a direct PostgreSQL runner. Until Supabase CLI's remote test command stops requiring Docker/Podman, the hosted-only path is the separate 40-assertion Auth/PostgREST suite; this distinction must remain visible rather than claiming the pgTAP suite executed.

## ADR-029 — Guarded persistent lead operations

The lead-to-discovery slice uses server-side reads and eight narrowly scoped authenticated database functions rather than direct browser writes or a service key. A tenant-scoped command ledger provides idempotency, lead versions and row locks protect concurrent edits, and activity is appended in the mutation transaction. Security-definer execution is deliberate: ordinary roles stay read-only, each function derives the actor from `auth.uid()` and rechecks membership, capability, ownership, transition and tenant relationships. The original eight functions were the beginning of the operation model, not authority for adjacent workflows. Later milestones implemented separate conversion, Client Book, review and referral boundaries; campaigns remain unavailable in Supabase mode pending their own reviewed operations.

## ADR-030 — Reproducible hosted operations and bounded reads

The hosted operation acceptance path is a checked-in Node PostgreSQL runner using a temporary TLS connection string and an explicit expected project reference. It wraps all verification data in one transaction, rolls back and independently checks zero residue. This suite verifies privileged operation internals; the REST suite separately verifies Auth/PostgREST RLS, while pgTAP remains a third schema-level suite and is never claimed without execution.

Persistent lead reads begin with the tenant-scoped lead selection and load related rows only by exact lead, contact or membership identifiers using explicit columns. Current-effective consent is selected by a shared deterministic rule: ignore future evidence, then prefer effective time, recorded time and immutable identifier in order. Missing and `Not recorded` remain non-affirmative states. Read indexes are added only for these concrete organisation, active-membership, lead-task and lead-appointment paths.

## ADR-031 — Separate guarded boundaries for Client Book and retention work

Conversion, household/contact/member maintenance, minimal-loan updates, review opportunity lifecycle and household tasks use domain-specific authenticated operations rather than widening the lead operation API. Each operation rechecks active membership, capability, ownership, lifecycle, tenant relationships, expected versions and checked idempotency inside the transaction. Archived households cannot be changed through ordinary maintenance or used to revive review work implicitly.

## ADR-032 — Bounded contact discovery and explicit maintenance authority

Client Book snapshots do not carry organisation-wide contact collections. Existing-contact selection uses a tenant-scoped, deterministic, capped search that requires both the household operation capability and `contact.view`. Manual household ownership defaults to self unless the actor also has explicit household-assignment authority. Duplicate preview is advisory, but final mutation authorization and duplicate validation remain authoritative.

## ADR-033 — Mortgage-owned referral partners and historical attribution

Referral partners remain in the mortgage vertical because relationship types and attribution rules are industry-specific. Persistent referral records use active Broker ownership, explicit Active/Inactive lifecycle, normalized duplicate review, expected versions and checked command idempotency. Exact normalized email blocks creation; normalized name/organisation matches require acknowledgment and never merge automatically. Inactivation preserves lead and household links. Active selectors contain only active partners, while targeted linked-record reads keep inactive historical attribution visible. Attribution is an operational record, not proof of causation, commission or revenue.

## ADR-034 — Campaign persistence remains a separate authorization boundary

The fixture campaign engine proves versioning, audience snapshots, conservative eligibility and reviewed-snapshot approval, but it does not authorize a direct persistence port. Persistent campaign drafting, submission, approval and cancellation require a separately reviewed operation model. Delivery, scheduling, queues, exports, provider credentials and outcome tracking remain out of scope unless independently authorised.

## Decisions before production

Production Supabase project configuration, invitation operations, MFA/recovery policy, tenant hierarchy, CRM ownership, calendar provider, messaging channels, consent policy, audit retention, data residency, encryption, duplicate/household resolution and production reporting definitions remain open.
